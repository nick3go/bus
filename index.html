IF YOU ONLY READ ONE THING

For a general-purpose ride, try an ordinary forward-facing seat in the front half, a little away from a door. For a short trip, choose an aisle seat near an exit. Leave priority seats and the wheelchair space available to people who need them.

04 / THE SUNNY SIDE

Left or right?
It depends on direction.

A tiny geometry tool for choosing shade.
Not a weather forecast or a route tracker.

Use the direction of the current road segment. Heading and sun bearing are inputs you choose. Buildings, clouds, season, tinted glass and turns can change actual exposure. Neither side stays shaded for an entire curving route.

06 / THE RULE THAT BEATS THE RANKING

A good seat is
one you can share.

  1. Follow the door signs. LPP’s published city guidance describes boarding at the first door and using the others to leave. Follow the driver’s instructions and any accessibility arrangements. [4, 5]
  2. Offer your seat when needed. Keep priority seats available to passengers who need them. Access needs may not be visible. [5]
  3. Keep the route through the bus clear. Bags belong with you, not on another seat. Do not block doors, the aisle or the designated wheelchair/stroller area.
  4. No seat available? Hold an appropriate handrail. Keep clear of door mechanisms, follow the on-board markings, and avoid moving around just to chase a better ranking. [5]
  5. Prepare to leave. Use the stop/door controls as marked. Allow people through and use a handhold when moving. The actual driver, markings and conditions outrank this guide.
07 / SOURCES & HOW TO READ THIS GUIDE

Useful advice.
Honest uncertainty.

What is sourced

Vehicle examples and accessibility equipment come from LPP and the City of Ljubljana. Door guidance and courtesy are supported by published LPP materials. Looking ahead and avoiding reading when motion sick are supported by CDC and NHS guidance.

What is our judgement

The zone comparisons, quietness proxies, friend-seating preferences and weighted recommendations are editorial rules of thumb. We have not measured noise, vibration, crowding or seat availability on LPP buses. There is no crash-safety ranking.

How the finder works

Each ordinary seating zone has an editorial 0–4 suitability rating on five criteria. Your sliders weight them. The score is 100 × weighted rating sum ÷ (4 × total weight). Short journeys add one unit of weight to exit access; long journeys add one unit to being away from doors. Equal scores keep the displayed zone order. Ratings and weights are visible below.

Inspect all ratings and the preset weights

The motion-sickness preset favours an available ordinary forward-facing front seat with a view ahead; it does not promise relief. Access needs bypass the ranking. A wheelchair or stroller bay is not an ordinary seat and is never scored.
